## ⛔ STRUCTURE EACH LESSON: SET THE STAGE → FILL THE MIDDLE → CONCLUDE (Joe locked, Ep10)
Joe's exact directive after the depth fix: *"set up the stage — what is this we are talking about;
in the middle fill it up; and in the end conclude and give either a solution or a proper statement,
don't leave people hanging."* And: make it SHORTER/tighter. So every day's caption follows:
1. **STAGE** — name the fear/topic plainly and set it with Joe's real trigger (Day 1: "The first
   fear that stopped me was money. I was looking at facilities priced at $800,000, $1.2 million,
   even $2 million, thinking, who on earth is going to hand me that?").
2. **MIDDLE** — the substance that gives real understanding: the belief-vs-truth shift, then the
   HOW in plain terms (DSCR explained so a beginner gets WHY the cushion matters, SBA as the
   down-payment answer). Each line pulls into the next; do not reset per paragraph.
3. **END** — land it with a payoff line + a firm conclusion or solution, never a hanging beat
   ("The money was never the barrier. The math was, and the math can be learned."), THEN the
   community CTA.
Tighter than the first draft — cut throat-clearing, keep the momentum. This structure is what turns
"put-together ideas" into "a flowing understood lesson."

## Gates that bit this session (all now in writing-craft — RUN THEM)
- **Bare-noun sweep (Rule 8):** Joe re-corrected after I'd already been told once. Pin
  self-storage on EVERY noun a stranger could misread — "lender"→"self-storage lender",
  "the deal"→"the storage deal", "the seller"→"the storage owner" (first use), "brokers"→
  "storage brokers" (first use), "the market"→"the self-storage market". Establish in the FIRST
  line; opener + CTA must never be bare; mid-body "the facility"/"the deal" is fine once locked.
- **DON'T over-apply the sweep to labels that were already fine.** I broke the Day-4 recap
  "The money. The seller. The broker." by changing "seller"→"self-storage owner" — Joe: "it
  should be the money the seller and the broker, this was already fine." When a word is a clear
  in-context LABEL (the three fears named by their day), leave it. The sweep is for nouns a cold
  stranger misreads, not for every occurrence.
- **Vocabulary / broken stitches (Rules 2,3):** Joe caught three in this batch — "They think it
  like this" (no grammar behind it → "The fear sounds like this"), "Not one is about money..."
  (clunky → "None of them are about..."), "every one disappears the moment you learn" (poor →
  "they all fall away the moment you learn"). These passed the dash/bare-noun scans but were
  never read cold. This is exactly why Rule 13 (final cold re-read) now exists.
- **Final cold re-read (Rule 13):** read the WHOLE assembled series back, line by line, out loud
  in your head, before handing to Joe. The mechanical scans are NOT a substitute.
