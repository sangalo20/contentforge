# Joe Downs — Short-Form Talking Topics (Joe records on camera)

Joe wants to record himself talking to camera for short-form (IG / TikTok / Facebook — same content to all three). He does NOT want a written script to read, and he does NOT want clever titles. He wants **topics pulled from the deeper understanding of the episode**, framed so he can just talk, each opening with clear context and finishing with a strong CTA.

This is a SEPARATE lane from:
- the **hook-wrapped real-clip promo videos** (ffmpeg, built from `/Promos` footage — `storagemoguls-podcast` + `brand-poster-design/references/hook-wrapped-real-clip.md`)
- the **educational posters** (`storagemoguls-podcast/references/educational-posters.md`)

Here Joe IS the video. We hand him topics + context + CTA; he records.

## ⛔ THE #1 RULE — CONTEXT FIRST, ALWAYS (Joe corrected this THREE times in one session)

This is the whole reason Joe is moving away from the old promo videos: **they dived straight into the insight with no setup, so the viewer had no idea what was being talked about and scrolled.** Joe's exact words across the session:

- *"Its not proper it just dived in without any context. The reason we are moving away from the other promo video is because they didnt really display any context within the video. We had no idea what was being talked about. The context should always be first and very clear so that as it continues it builds towards a nice conclusion. The reader should never feel like they are struggling to flow."*
- *"Where is the context of this????"* (said when topics were framed as conclusions/titles instead of leading with setup)

### What "context" actually means here
The context is **the plain-language ground a COLD STRANGER stands on before the topic makes sense.** It is NOT the insight, NOT the takeaway, NOT a headline. It's the setup that orients someone who knows nothing.

A topic title like *"Why the reports the bank doesn't require matter most"* is a CONCLUSION — it assumes the viewer already knows what due diligence is, what the bank orders, why it matters. That FAILS Joe's test.

The fix is to open on something a total stranger already understands, THEN move to the point:
- ✅ "When you buy a storage facility, the bank makes you get a bunch of reports before they'll lend. Most people assume if the bank didn't ask for something, they don't need it. That assumption is what gets people hurt." → THEN the insight.
- ✅ "Every buyer needs an attorney to close a deal. And almost everyone reaches for the one they already know — a cousin, the guy who did their house closing." → THEN why that's wrong.
- ✅ "The letter of intent is the very first thing you sign when you go after a deal. Almost everybody's been told it's non-binding, so they treat it like it barely matters." → THEN the leverage point.
- ✅ "At the end of every deal there's a document called the settlement statement. It lists every cost and who's covering it. Most first-time buyers assume it's fixed." → THEN what's negotiable.

Each opens on a fact the viewer already accepts (the bank makes you get reports / everybody knows a lawyer / the LOI is the first thing you sign / there's a settlement statement at the end) so nobody is lost. The arc is always: **plain context → build → clean conclusion.** The viewer should never struggle to follow.

## ⛔ NO CLEVER TITLES — topics come from the DEEPER UNDERSTANDING (Joe, this session)

Joe: *"they should not be based on weird titles but based on the deeper understanding of what was being talked about."*

Don't invent catchy names ("The $10K Mistake", "Stop Using Your Brother-in-Law" as a headline). Name the topic plainly by what it actually IS ("Choosing the right attorney", "What a letter of intent really does", "Who really pays the closing costs"). The value is the substance of the conversation, framed so Joe can talk — not a marketing title.

Pull topics from the episode's **Understanding** (the Notion Episode Library page) + the transcript, the same Understanding-first discipline as `storagemoguls-podcast`. Read the whole understanding, pick the 3-5 richest self-contained ideas, and frame each as something Joe knows cold and can riff on.

## ⛔ STRONG CTAs — earn the click, never "link in the bio" alone (Joe corrected: "The CTA is short and weak")

A weak CTA is a bare "link's in the bio" — same flat line on every video, gives the viewer no reason to go. Joe rejected that.

A STRONG CTA has three beats:
1. **A reason / payoff** tied to THIS video's specific topic — what they'll get if they watch ("the mistakes that end deals almost never show up on the bank's checklist… we go through exactly where they hide").
2. **The episode** — "Full episode's live now on YouTube" (drives the curious).
3. **The community / funnel** — a warm, earned invite to storagemoguls.ai, tied to the viewer's own next step ("if you want to actually learn how to vet a deal like this, come join us at storagemoguls.ai").

This is the same two-step friction-free road map as the SM brand captions: episode for the curious, storagemoguls.ai for the ready. Warm, not salesy. Vary the wording per topic so the set doesn't read as assembled.

## ⛔ BANNED PHRASES still apply (from storagemoguls-podcast + user profile)

Joe re-flagged these as "wrong vocabulary, not even good English" — DO NOT use in CTAs or copy:
`read a deal like an owner` / `go from wondering to owning` / `run one for themselves` / `Follow + listen` / `lands in your feed` / `Follow us so…`.
When one slips in, swap it and FLAG the swap to Joe (this session: "read a deal like an owner" → "learn to actually spot where you're overpaying"). Name the subject explicitly — "self-storage investing", never bare "storage" — for cold strangers.

## ⛔ IF SCRIPTS ARE ASKED FOR: write how Joe TALKS, not written copy chopped into lines

Joe's default here is TOPICS (talking prompts), not scripts. But if he asks for actual scripts/word-for-word, the failure mode to avoid (he caught it this session — *"Sounds off"*, then *"yes"* to loosening) is **headline-stacking**: short punchy sentences machine-gunned with no breath, caption fragments that no human says out loud ("A $1.5M building, suddenly worth six hundred grand").

To fix, read it ALOUD in your head and make it sound like Joe talking:
- Use contractions (it's, you're, that's, gonna).
- Add the connective tissue of real speech ("so", "but here's the thing", "because", "look").
- Soften numbers to spoken form ("about a million and a half", not "$1.5 million").
- Vary rhythm — not every sentence lands like a headline. Let some breathe.
- Run the `humanizer` skill (zero em/en dashes, kill "not X it's Y", rule-of-three, -ing tails).

But lead with TOPICS unless Joe explicitly wants scripts. He landed on topics as the final format this session.

## Length / format target (Joe's spec this session)
- **20-30 seconds** per video when scripts are involved; topics themselves should be riffable to that length.
- Same content to IG + TikTok + Facebook.
- 3-4 core topics, with an optional 5th marked clearly as optional.

## Output = a Word document

Joe asked for the final deliverable as a `.docx`. Structure per topic:

```
Topic N: <plain-language name of what it IS>
CONTEXT (open with this)   → the cold-stranger setup, 2-3 sentences
WHAT TO TALK ABOUT         → the build + the real insight from the understanding
CTA (finish strong)        → reason/payoff → full episode on YouTube → warm storagemoguls.ai invite
```

Plus a short header block: brand + episode + guest, and a one-line note that Joe records each freely, context-first.

## End-to-end workflow
1. Read the episode **Understanding** (Notion Episode Library page for the episode) + transcript if needed — same source discipline as `storagemoguls-podcast`. Never write topics from memory.
2. Pick the 3-5 richest self-contained ideas. Name each PLAINLY (no clever titles).
3. For EACH, write: CONTEXT (cold-stranger setup first) → WHAT TO TALK ABOUT (build to the insight) → strong two-step CTA (reason → YouTube → storagemoguls.ai).
4. Scan for banned phrases; swap + flag any that slip in. Name the subject as "self-storage investing".
5. Build the `.docx` and deliver via `MEDIA:`.
6. Offer (don't assume) to add caption + on-screen hook per topic. Do NOT schedule anything — this is Joe's record-it-himself lane; he shoots the videos.

## Pitfalls (all learned the hard way this session)
- Framing a topic as a CONCLUSION/title instead of leading with context = the #1 rejection. Open on ground a stranger already stands on.
- Weak repeated "link in the bio" CTA = rejected. Every CTA earns the click with a topic-specific reason + payoff + two destinations.
- Headline-stacked "scripts" that read like chopped copy = rejected. If scripting, write how Joe talks (contractions, connective tissue, spoken numbers, varied rhythm) and humanize.
- Clever marketing titles = rejected. Plain names from the real substance.
- Don't jump to scripts when Joe wants topics; don't jump to scheduling — Joe records these himself.
