## What This Skill Does

Joe records short-form videos (YouTube Shorts, IG Reels, FB Reels) to drive cold viewers to his **free live Monday-night Storage 101 call**. The *bodies* of these shorts are usually strong. The **CTA tail** — the last 2 to 4 sentences — is where they fail: a vague "free weekly call, link in bio" that assumes the viewer already knows what Storage 101 is, what happens on it, and what to click.

This skill owns the CTA tail. It makes the ask **so clear and the direction so concrete that a complete non-tech beginner can follow it** — exactly the bar Joe set (Jun 2026): *"make the CTA so clear and the direction so clear that even a non-tech person would be able to follow."*

---

## The Core Principle — Joe is the INVITE, and the invite still CLOSES

This came straight from Alvin's feedback (Jun 2026) and Joe agreed — but Joe sharpened it hard afterward, so read both halves:

- **Joe's personal brand = the teacher, the invite, "one of the right people in the room."** Come-join-me, come-learn-with-me. Warm. The hard SELLING (price tables, urgency, "buy now") lives on the funnel pages / community / other accounts, never in Joe's mouth. When Joe sounds like a pitchman, it breaks the brand.
- **⚠️ Inviting/teacher does NOT mean it can't close (Joe corrected this directly, Jun 2026).** Warm and "makes the ask" are NOT opposites. Joe: *"Just because Joe's voice is inviting and teacher it does not mean it can't close."* The teacher tone is HOW Joe closes, not a reason to soften the ask into mush. A good teacher still says "here's exactly what to do, do it now." So: earn it with warmth, THEN make a real, confident, specific ask. Do not let "warm" decay into a weak, optional-sounding invitation ("if you ever feel like it, maybe check it out"). The close is direct: *"Tap the link, register with your name, and I'll see you Monday."* That IS the teacher closing.
- The CTA's job is to **bridge** a cold viewer to one easy next step AND get them to take it. Bridge + close, not bridge instead of close.

> The distinction that matters: salesY (pushy, pressure, hype) = off-brand for Joe. CLOSING (a clear, warm, confident ask for one specific action) = required. Joe can close all day; he just closes like a teacher, not a closer.

---

## The Problem Being Fixed (so you never reintroduce it)

The old tail, *"...a free weekly call called Storage 101. Link in bio."*, raises three silent questions in a beginner's head, and answers none:

1. **What even is Storage 101?** (What happens on it?)
2. **Why would I want to go?** (What's in it for me, and is it risky/expensive?)
3. **What exactly do I do?** (Click what? Then what happens?)

A good CTA tail answers all three in plain words.

---

## The 4-Part CTA Tail Formula

Every CTA tail must hit these four beats, in plain language, in this order:

1. **NAME IT + say what actually happens.** Not just "Storage 101", say what the call *is*: *"a free live call I run every Monday night called Storage 101. The first 15 minutes I explain what self storage is from scratch, then we dig into one real topic."* (The 15-min overview + rotating topic is the real format, describe it, don't assume it.)
2. **REMOVE THE RISK.** Say the price and the barrier out loud: *"It's free, there's no homework, and you don't need to know a thing going in."* Beginners need permission to show up unprepared.
3. **ONE LITERAL ACTION, with name capture.** Exactly one instruction, concrete: *"Tap the link, register with your name, and you're in."* The name-capture is deliberate, Alvin flagged that capturing details lets the team remind/follow up (someone who watches Thursday can be pulled to Monday). Default action is **"tap the link in bio / on screen."**
4. **WARM CLOSE that actually CLOSES.** The invite tone, but a real ask, not a soft maybe: *"Come learn it with me. I'll see you Monday night."* Warm + confident + specific. (Inviting ≠ weak — see Core Principle. Don't end on "if you ever feel like it.")

If a tail is missing any of the four, it's not done.

## Hook / Angle Library

Mix any **hook style** with any **audience angle**. The body earns the click; the tail (formula above) closes it. Joe's 5x3 matrix = up to 15 genuinely different promos for the *same* Monday call, rotate fresh variants instead of reposting one (see Distribution note).

### The 3 audience angles (Joe named these)
- **Tired of the 9-to-5** — "you've heard about self storage, not sure it's for you, want out of the job."
- **Tired landlord** — toilets, tenants, trash; wants to transition out of residential headaches into storage.
- **High earner / tax-benefit seeker** — makes good money, not tired of the job, wants real-estate tax advantages without being a landlord.

### The 5 proven hook styles (from the working scripts)
1. **Reframe** — "No toilets. No trash. No tenants. That's self storage in three words."
2. **Fear / objection** — "You don't need to know anything about storage to get started. I didn't."
3. **Surprising stat** — a real storage-demand figure. ⚠️ The correct penetration number is roughly **1 in 10 / ~10% of US households** rents self storage, NOT "1 in 3 Americans" (Joe rejected the inflated 1-in-3 / 1-in-17 framing, Jun 2026 — see Factual Gate). If you don't have a number you can source THIS session, drop the stat and hook on the *direction* ("demand has climbed for years, storage holds up in downturns") with a `VERIFY` flag, rather than ship a precise number you can't stand behind.
4. **Comparison** — storage vs. residential ("your rental property is lying to you").
5. **Why now / opportunity** — institutional buyers slowing acquisitions opens a window for individuals. ⚠️ This is a MARKET-TIMING claim in Joe's mouth, it was solidly true in 2023 to 2024 but ages fast. Confirm it reflects the CURRENT market before Joe says "right now" on camera, or frame it as a general pattern not a present-tense fact (see Factual Gate).

## Format Spec (match the working template)

- **Length:** ~75 to 80 words total, ~27 to 29 second read. Tight.
- **On-screen text overlay (first 3 seconds):** one punchy line = the hook. (e.g. "No toilets. No trash. No tenants.")
- **Structure:** Hook (3-sec overlay + spoken) -> Body/Bridge (plain-language why, earns the click) -> CTA tail (the 4-part formula).
- **The bridge is mandatory.** Before the CTA, the body must make a beginner understand *why* in plain words, no jargon, no assumed knowledge. Alvin's bar: dumb it down enough that someone who knows nothing about storage follows it.
- Deliver each script with: the overlay line, the full spoken script, estimated read time, and which hook/angle it uses (mirrors how Gisele formats them).

## Voice + Humanize Gate (same hard rule as audience-comment-replies)

Joe's standing rule applies to ALL his copy, spoken included:

- **ZERO em/en dashes** in the final script. Joe checks this first and treats a stray dash as proof the humanize pass didn't happen. Replace with a period or comma, or split the sentence. Do a literal find for the dash characters before handing it over.
- **Straight quotes only** (`"` `'`), never curly.
- **No hype words** ("incredible," "game-changing"), no rule-of-three padding, no "it's not just X, it's Y," no AI-stiff phrasing.
- **Short, declarative sentences.** First person. A real person talking, not a script being read.
- Load the `humanizer` skill for the full pattern checklist on anything longer than a couple of sentences.

## FACTUAL GATE (hard rule — applies to the BODY, not just the tail)

This skill exists in the shadow of the fabricated-origin-story incident (Jun 2026). On the very first CTA pass I built, Joe caught factual problems in the BODIES, so the gate covers the whole script, not just the CTA tail. **Run every one of these before handing over:**

1. **Body claims must match Joe's REAL history (soul.md is the source of truth).** Joe's background is **commercial real estate**, not residential. A draft said "I've been in residential real estate since 2003"; Joe's actual path is financial advisor → bought the bar → commercial real estate (~$90M, 1031 syndications) → 2008 → distressed debt → storage ~2016. Three pitfalls this caught:
   - Don't write "residential" when his lane is commercial.
   - Don't let scripts CONTRADICT each other (one said "residential since 2003," another said "my background is commercial real estate, not storage" — pick the true one, which is commercial, and keep it consistent across the batch).
   - **Joe was NOT a residential landlord, so the toilets/trash/tenants/midnight-dog-call/11pm-flood pains can NEVER be his OWN lived experience (Joe corrected this Jun 2026).** Those are residential-landlord pains and only work as a CONTRAST against residential, never as something Joe personally went through. Worse, pairing "I've been in COMMERCIAL real estate" with "nobody's dog tears up MY unit" is incoherent — commercial has no dog/tenant either. Two safe framings: (a) OBSERVE that storage avoids those headaches ("nobody lives in a storage unit, no midnight calls, no tenant trashing the place") — Joe commenting on the asset, not claiming the pain; (b) EXPLICIT contrast ("compared to renting out homes, storage is dead simple, no dog tearing up the carpet") — openly comparing storage TO residential, which is honest. Save the explicit residential-contrast for the storage-vs-residential script (#4), which already owns that framing.
   - If an angle needs an origin/first-deal/motivation detail you don't have the REAL version of, write the point without inventing specifics or ASK Joe ("have you ever owned residential rentals yourself?") — never fill the gap with invention or guess his history. (Same rule as the brand-knowledge-hub Factual Integrity Gate.)

2. **Stats must be real and sourceable, never inflated for punch.** The storage penetration figure is ~**1 in 10 / ~10% of households**, NOT "1 in 3 Americans." An inflated-but-plausible number is still a fabrication. If you can't source a number THIS session, cut it and hook on the defensible direction, with a `VERIFY` flag for Joe/team to fill in the real figure. **The verified stats bank (real numbers + sources + the curl-around-CAPTCHA verify path) lives in `references/storage-stats.md` — pull from there.**

3. **Market-timing claims must be CURRENT.** "Institutional buyers are pulling back right now" was true in 2023–2024; whether it's true "right now" depends on today's market. A present-tense market claim in Joe's mouth ages badly. Either confirm it's current or reframe as a general pattern. Flag `VERIFY`.

4. **Storage reality check** (inherited from brand-knowledge-hub): month-to-month leases are UNIVERSAL and normal in storage, never a risk. Don't leak generic real-estate diligence language into storage copy.

5. **No INSIDER SHORTHAND — a true fact stated cryptically still fails the beginner bar (Joe corrected Jun 2026).** A claim can be fully fact-checked and still be wrong FOR THE AUDIENCE if it assumes knowledge the cold viewer doesn't have. Joe caught "the sector even held up through 2008" and asked *"what happened in 2008 for it to be significant?"* — the 5.1%-return figure is real, but "2008" is insider shorthand for the financial crisis that only lands if you already know it was the crash. These shorts are FOR total beginners (see the non-tech-person bar at the top), so any year, acronym, ticker, named REIT, "1031," "cap rate," "NOI," "the crisis," etc. must either be spelled out plainly or cut. Two fixes (Joe-validated pattern): (a) NAME IT — "it even held up during the 2008 financial crisis, when almost everything else in real estate got hammered"; or (b) DROP THE DATE, keep the idea — "storage tends to hold up even when the economy doesn't, that's rare for any investment." Default to (b) in a short: it makes the same true point without making the viewer stop and decode a reference. Dates/tickers are texture that helps an investor audience but slow down the cold beginner these shorts target. Run a literal pass: for every proper noun, year, or industry term, ask "would someone who knows nothing about storage or investing understand this without stopping?" If not, plain-language it or cut it.

6. **Be honest about what you verified.** If you can't run web_search this session, SAY you flagged rather than confirmed a stat — don't assert confidence you don't have. Flagging beats guessing.

7. **NO INVENTED DRAMATIC SPECIFICS, and NO OVERCLAIMS (Joe corrected this repeatedly, Jun 2026 — this is the pattern I keep failing).** Fact-checking is not only about stats. The recurring failure mode: writing a punchy line that's *plausible* but *invented* or *exaggerated* to make the hook hit harder. Joe caught three in one batch and the lesson is bigger than any one fix:
   - **Invented specifics:** \"your tenant floods the bathroom at 11pm and your cash flow's gone for six months.\" The 11pm and the \"six months\" are made up for drama; a clogged toilet does not erase six months of cash flow. Joe: *\"this seems unrealistic.\"* Never manufacture a specific scenario, time, or dollar/duration figure for color. State the real, modest truth (residential has tenants, repairs, vacancies that interrupt income) without theatrics.
   - **Superlatives / sweeping claims:** \"storage is the simplest model I've come across\" asserts a career-wide judgment for Joe that can't be backed. Joe: *\"I don't think saying ... is correct.\"* When a bridge needs a claim you can't stand behind, DROP IT — go straight from the setup into the plain facts and let them carry it (Joe: *\"I think we should just drop it\"*). \"Here's what stands out about storage\" then the facts beats any superlative.
   - **The deeper rule: I should CATCH these myself, not wait for Joe to flag them one at a time.** Joe: *\"Are you really learning? You should be catching these things.\"* Before handing over, run a literal scan of every body line and ask: \"Is this a real, modest, defensible statement, or did I punch it up with an invented detail / superlative / dramatic scenario?\" If punched up, deflate it to the truth or cut it. Default to the understated true version; the facts are strong enough without embellishment.

8. **FRAMING must point at the OWNER's benefit, not at what the owner does TO people (Joe corrected #4, Jun 2026).** A line can be true and still repel the audience. \"You raise rates with a text. If they don't pay, you auction the unit and move on\" is accurate, but it makes Joe sound like he's describing leverage OVER tenants — cold, even predatory — and points the viewer's imagination at the person getting their stuff auctioned, not at themselves as a smart owner. Joe: *\"quite a risky thing to say ... I dont think it would attract people.\"* For a warm, come-learn-with-me invite, the benefits must land ON the prospective owner (\"lighter lift,\" \"no one to manage,\" \"nothing to fix at midnight\") and PULL them in (\"which is exactly why so many people are drawn to it\"), never describe the owner exercising power over a tenant. This is consistent with the Core Principle: invite, don't sound like an operator squeezing customers. Run this check on every comparison/why-now script: does the benefit land on the viewer, or is it aimed at a tenant/customer? Re-aim it at the viewer.

CTAs and bodies reference only what is real: a real **free** Monday-night call, real format (15-min overview + topic), real numbers. If a claim isn't verifiable, cut it or flag `VERIFY`, never invent.

## ⚠️ THE OVERCLAIM TRAP — stop writing punchy specifics you can't back (Joe corrected this 4+ times, Jun 2026)

This is the single most repeated mistake on this skill. I kept writing lines that are *vivid* but *unsupported*, and Joe had to catch each one. The pattern: reaching for a punchy detail and inventing the specifics to make it land. **Every one of these is a fabrication even though it "sounds right."** Run this as its own pass on every script.

**The actual misses Joe caught (learn the SHAPE, not just these instances):**
1. **Superlatives about Joe's judgment.** "storage is the simplest model I've come across" — an unprovable sweeping claim about his whole career. CUT. (We dropped the claim entirely; "here's what stands out about storage" then let the facts carry it.)
2. **Invented dramatic scenarios + fake numbers.** "your tenant floods the bathroom at 11pm and your cash flow's gone for six months" — the 11pm flood is invented theater and "six months" is a made-up figure. A clogged toilet doesn't kill six months of income. CUT the specifics; say the true general thing ("tenants, repairs, vacancies, the calls when something breaks").
3. **The wrong-lens / off-putting angle.** Describing the OWNER doing things TO people ("raise rates with a text," "auction the unit and move on") reads cold/predatory to a cold viewer and works against the warm invite. Reframe so benefits land ON THE OWNER ("lighter lift, no one to manage, nothing to fix at midnight"), as a PULL ("which is exactly why so many people are drawn to it"), never as leverage over a tenant.
4. **Promising call CONTENT the call may not deliver.** A CTA said Storage 101 covers "the whole comparison, storage versus residential, every Monday" — but Joe doesn't know that specific comparison is on the agenda. **The CTA must describe ONLY the call's real every-week format (15-min storage overview + one rotating topic). NEVER promise the call covers the specific topic of that short.** The short's topic ≠ the call's agenda.

**The standing rule (apply to BOTH body and CTA, every script):**
- **No invented specifics.** No named scenarios, no times ("11pm"), no dollar/duration figures, no superlatives about Joe — unless it's verified or Joe said it. If you want texture, use a TRUE general statement, not invented color.
- **No comparative/superlative claim** ("simplest," "best," "easiest") unless it's defensible AND Joe would actually say it. When in doubt, cut the claim and let the plain facts stand.
- **Keep the lens on the viewer-as-owner and the tone warm/invitational** — never describe the owner exercising power over a tenant.
- **The CTA promises only what's true EVERY week** (the free Monday call + its overview-plus-one-topic format). Never let the CTA imply the call is "about" the short's subject.
- **Catch these BEFORE Joe does.** Before handing over, literally re-read each sentence and ask: "Can I back this exact claim? Did Joe say it, or did I make it vivid?" If made-up, cut or generalize. Joe's standard: I should be catching these, not him.

## Distribution Note (context only, not this skill's job)

Alvin proposed reposting promo shorts every few weeks, judged on sign-ups not views. The **durable principle is right**: promos are measured by registrations, and evergreen promos should run continuously. But the **delete-and-re-upload mechanic is the weak version**, it throws away the asset's accumulated views/engagement and algorithmic learning, the "freshness" boost is largely a myth, and repeated identical re-uploads can read as spam. **Better mechanic, same goal:** rotate FRESH variants from the 5x3 matrix (real new shots at different people), put small paid spend behind the variant that converts, and track registrations per variant. Cadence/repost decisions belong in `metricool-scheduling` once tested, do not enshrine the delete-repost tactic anywhere until it's proven.

## Hard Stops (rewrite any script containing these)
- A vague tail ("free call, link in bio") that doesn't say what the call is, what it costs, or exactly what to click.
- Salesy/hard-sell tone in Joe's mouth (that belongs on the funnel pages).
- Missing any of the 4 CTA beats (name+what / remove risk / one literal action+name capture / warm close).
- Em/en dashes or curly quotes in the final script.
- Any fabricated benefit, stat, or feature; an inflated-but-plausible stat (like "1 in 3 Americans") counts as fabrication.
- A body claim that contradicts Joe's real history (he is COMMERCIAL real estate, not residential) or contradicts another script in the same batch.
- A present-tense market-timing claim that hasn't been confirmed current.
- An INVENTED dramatic specific (a made-up time like \"11pm,\" a made-up duration/dollar figure like \"six months of cash flow gone\") or a SUPERLATIVE/sweeping claim Joe can't back (\"the simplest model I've come across\"). Deflate to the modest truth or cut. (Rule 7.)
- A benefit aimed AT a tenant/customer rather than at the prospective owner (\"raise rates with a text, auction the unit\") — sounds predatory, repels the audience. Re-aim every benefit at the viewer. (Rule 8.)
- More than one action in the CTA (confuses the beginner) or jargon with no bridge.
