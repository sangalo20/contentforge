# Teaching-Caption Structure (the STRUCTURE layer for educational posts)

Joe approved this spine on the SM Ep10 4-day series (July 2026) after rejecting drafts that
"dived in without setting up what we're talking about" and "left people hanging." It sits ON TOP
of the writing-craft + humanizer gates, it does not replace them. Use it for any single-lesson
educational caption (LinkedIn / Instagram / Facebook long form).

## The core rule (fixes both failures at once)
1. **The POINT never appears before the HAND-OFF line.** Everything above the hand-off is framing
   (what is this, who is it for, why care). Diving straight to the lesson = the reader has no slot
   to put it in. This is the "it just dived in without anything" failure Joe named.
2. **The last non-CTA line is always a CLOSE** (a solution or a firm summary), NEVER the problem
   restated and never a hollow comfort ("it's fixable"). This is the "don't leave people hanging"
   rule (also writing-craft Rule 10).
3. **The CTA is separate from the close.** The lesson must feel finished BEFORE the invite. The CTA
   is a warm invite, not the conclusion.

## The spine (myth-busting / reframe lessons — Joe's usual shape)
Topic → Belief (validated) → Turn → Truth + Mechanism → Proof → Firm Close → CTA.

1. **STAGE-SET / HOOK (open):** name the arena and the reader in it, and the stakes. Not warm-up
   throat-clearing, every line already teaches. e.g. "Most people who want to buy their first
   self-storage facility never do. Not because they can't. Because of three quiet fears."
2. **HAND-OFF (1 line):** point forward without spending the point. A flat declarative, NEVER a
   rhetorical question. e.g. "Today, the loudest one. Money."
3. **THE BELIEF, VALIDATED:** state the misconception in the reader's own words AND acknowledge why
   it's understandable (keeps them on your side, never mock it). e.g. "Here is how it sounds in your
   head... I know it well, it was my first thought too."
4. **THE TURN (1 line):** mark the pivot plainly. e.g. "But that is not what a lender is actually
   looking at."
5. **TRUTH + MECHANISM:** the reframe in one clear line, then immediately the WHY that makes it real
   (the correction without the mechanism is just an assertion; the mechanism is the teaching). e.g.
   "A lender funds the deal, not you," then DSCR/SBA explained in plain English.
6. **CONCRETE PROOF:** one small real number or situation that shows the reframe working.
7. **FIRM CLOSE:** resolve the exact tension the opener created, in a complete standalone sentence
   the reader could screenshot. Solution close ("here's the actual move") OR firm-summary close
   ("the money is not what's standing in your way, the math is, and the math can be learned").
8. **CTA (separate, warm):** the community/call invite. Not the conclusion.

## Word budget (~150-220 words for a long-form caption)
Stage-set + hand-off ~40-55 words · Belief + Turn + Truth + Proof ~80-120 · Close ~20-35 · CTA ~20-30.

## The two tests
- **Cold-open test:** does the reader know what this is and why it matters BEFORE the point lands?
  If the point is in the first line, it dived in cold. Rewrite so framing comes first.
- **Last-line test:** read only the final non-CTA sentence, cold. Does the lesson feel landed, or
  like you just stopped talking? If it trails, rewrite into a solution or firm summary.

## Frameworks this draws from (for reference)
- **BLUF** (bottom line up front) — but the "bottom line" for a teaching post is the FRAMED main
  idea, not the bare punchline, or you dive in cold.
- **Context → Point → Payoff** — the minimum viable spine.
- **Hook → Context → Insight → Application → Takeaway** — the full teaching arc (maps to the spine above).
- Borrow the inverted pyramid's front-loading, but NOT its weak tail (that's the hanging failure).
