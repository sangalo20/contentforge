# SKILL.md — Writing Craft (shared foundation)

**Skill ID:** writing-craft
**Owner:** Joe Downs (applies to ALL brands)
**Role:** The "write it properly" gate — runs on every piece of content, every brand, BEFORE the humanizer pass.
**Created:** June 2026 (Joe directive — same class of error kept recurring across Joe's content AND Successful Idiots; the fix had to live in ONE shared place, not be re-patched per skill)

---

## Why this skill exists (read first)

Three different things govern good content, and they are NOT the same:

| Layer | Owns | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | Sounds like *this person/brand* (Joe's flat declaratives, SI's tone, etc.) | per-brand skill |
| **Writing craft** | The English is **correct, clear, and actually means something** | **THIS skill** |
| **Humanizer** | Doesn't read like AI slop (dashes, rule-of-three, -ing tails…) | `humanizer` |

A sentence can pass voice and humanizer and still be **broken writing** — wrong word, mis-paraphrased source, words stitched together that don't parse out of context. Voice and the de-AI pass do not catch that. This gate does.

Joe stated the root problem directly (June 2026): *"you're not inferring to what was being said, you're just placing the words there… sometimes it sounds tacky and sometimes it doesn't make sense."* The canonical example: a podcast guest says an address like **"the 46th"** in flowing speech — lifting "the 46th" into a post is meaningless to a reader; a human would infer and write **"they just want your address"** (or the clear equivalent). That inference is the job.

This is brand-agnostic ON PURPOSE. Fix a writing rule here once and it lands for Joe, Storage Moguls, Successful Idiots, and every future brand — instead of being re-fixed in one skill and forgotten in the others (which is exactly what kept happening).

## The Rules

### 1. Infer meaning when the surface text isn't self-clear — selectively, not always
Speech carries meaning through tone, pacing, and a long shared context. Writing has to stand on its own in a short space. When a lifted phrase would NOT carry its meaning to a reader who didn't hear the episode, **infer what was actually meant and write that.**

- ❌ "He said he just needs the 46th." (meaningless out of context — it was a spoken address)
- ✅ "He just needs your address." (the inferred, clear meaning)

**Selective, not total.** Do NOT rewrite everything — only the parts that are genuinely unclear standing alone. Clear lines stay as they are. Over-inferring invents; under-inferring leaves nonsense. Judge each unclear span on its own.

### 2. Never stitch words together that don't parse
LLM assembly produces phrases that *sound* plausible but don't actually hold together when read literally. Read every clause as a literal reader would. If two pieces were joined only because they "sounded right," and the result doesn't parse, rewrite it as a real sentence.

- Test: read the clause cold, no surrounding context. Does it mean one clear thing? If not, it's stitched — fix it.

### 3. Right vocabulary — the correct word, not a near-neighbor
Use the word that actually means the thing. Not a synonym that's *close*, not a fancier register word, not a made-up word. (Joe's recurring catch: "rang out" ≠ an unanswered phone; check what a word actually means before using it.)

- No invented words. No words used in the wrong sense. No upgrading "stuff" to "elements" to sound smarter.
- When unsure a word means what you think, pick the plain correct word instead.
- Catches from real sessions: "rang out" ≠ an unanswered phone; "a text that remembered you" (the text REMINDS you, doesn't remember you — check the verb against its subject); "to get back in front of you" vs the truer "to remind you" (pick the verb that names the actual mechanism, not a vaguer near-synonym).

### 4. Faithful paraphrase, not transcription — and not invention
Two valid moves when pulling from a source:
- **Quote/render what was genuinely said** when it's already clear: *"The poster reads: NOI is effective gross income minus operating expenses."* — fine if the speaker said it.
- **Paraphrase to clarify** the parts that are unclear standing alone — say what they MEANT in words a reader understands.

Never a third move: do not transcribe an unclear fragment verbatim and hope context saves it, and do not invent a claim the source never made. (Truth gate still applies — paraphrasing clarifies meaning, it does not add facts.)

### 5. It must read clean cold
Final test for every line: a reader who never heard the source and doesn't know the backstory reads this line by itself — does it make complete, correct sense? If no, it fails this gate. Rewrite before moving to the humanizer pass.

### 6. Sentence-to-sentence logic — each line must answer/advance the one before it (Joe corrected HARD, June 2026, applies to ALL brands)

The most-repeated correction class: a next sentence that *sounds* like it follows but doesn't *logically* follow from the specific thing the previous sentence set up. Smooth cadence is NOT connection — two lines can flow sonically and still not connect. Joe's words: *"you are still just attaching things."*

- **If the prior sentence posed a QUESTION, the next answers THAT question, on its own terms.** Worked example: copy quoting a text that asks *"want your usual ready for Thursday at noon?"* must be followed by an answer to the offer (*"Whether you say yes that day or not…"*) — NOT *"Maybe you go, maybe you don't"*, which answers a different question (did you visit) nobody asked. The text asked to SCHEDULE, not to come in. Match the response to the actual question.
- **Never add a clause that NEGATES/reverses the beat it lands.** \"They put themselves back in your head\" + \"you didn't leave on purpose\" — the second clause drags back to the leaving and undercuts the forward point. Cut anything that reverses the momentum of the sentence it attaches to.
- **Test:** trace the LOGICAL thread end-to-end, sentence by sentence. For each line ask *does this answer or advance the EXACT thing the previous line set up?* If it answers an adjacent question, sits beside the prior line without connecting, or reverses it — rewrite.
- **Don't stack 3 fragments for rhythm** (\"No billboard. No ad budget. A text that…\"). Staccato list-cadence reads constructed/AI; write connected sentences and let at most one deliberate fragment stand.

This is the sentence-level sibling of Rule 3 (word-level correctness): Rule 3 = the right *word*, Rule 6 = the right *connection*. Both fail the same way — sounds right, isn't right.

### 7. Lead with the INSIGHT; relatability and stats SUPPORT, never gate (Joe corrected, June 2026, applies to ALL brands)

Separate from correctness — this is about what a post FOREGROUNDS. Joe flagged it as a general content problem ("our content isn't really getting relatable to our audience"). Two linked failures:

- **Never position a whole post on an industry-specific number.** A stat that's true for ONE industry (e.g. a storage-specific "$800 per missed call") silently tells every other reader "not me," and makes the post about the statistic instead of the idea. Lead with the universal INSIGHT in plain words; demote the number to at most ONE supporting line on ONE platform (usually the long-form body), explicitly subordinated ("the number isn't really the point"), or cut it. NEVER the headline; NEVER the poster hero.
- **Lead with the example that mirrors the READER's own world, not the author's.** If the audience is mixed (any small business), open with the everyday case they live (the text you missed at dinner, your favorite shop reaching back out), and treat the author's home industry as just one example among several. The reader has to SEE THEMSELVES in the first line.
- **The visual obeys the same rule.** A poster must hero the MESSAGE, not a big `$NNN`. If a poster leads with an industry stat for a universal insight, rebuild it insight-first. (And the "didn't X, you Y" negative-parallelism is an AI tell on poster copy too, not just captions — cut it from baked-in art the same as from prose.)

Worked example + brand-specific framing: `successfulidiots-podcast` → "INSIGHT LEADS, STATS SUPPORT" (SI Ep031: rebuilt a `$800+` big-stat poster into a typographic insight poster). The rule itself is brand-agnostic and lives here.

### 8. MESSAGE CLARITY is the #1 priority — the stranger test (Joe corrected repeatedly, SI Ep16 July 2026, ALL brands)

This is the single most-repeated correction across every session. Above voice, above cleverness, above polish: **the message must be so clear that a total stranger, scrolling with zero context and no knowledge of the episode, instantly gets what's being passed.** If they have to stop and decode it, it has already failed — most people just scroll on.

- **The message is the backbone.** When Joe says "the message should be the backbone of every piece," he means the ONE thing the piece is passing must be obvious at a glance, on the art itself and in the first line of copy — not buried under content, not implied, not hidden behind a clever label.
- **Clever labels fail the stranger test.** "The middle disappears," "scales to zero," "you hate the rate," "train it once" — these read as riddles to someone who wasn't in the room. State the actual thing in plain words instead. (Joe rejected each of these by name.)
- **The glance test:** read the piece as someone who has never heard of the show and knows nothing about the topic. Can they, in about a second, say "I understand what this is telling me"? If not, rewrite until they can.
- This sits above Rule 5 (reads clean cold): Rule 5 is "the sentence parses"; Rule 8 is "the POINT lands instantly." A line can parse and still not deliver a clear message fast enough.
- **NAME THE TRANSITION, don't make the reader decode "the next one" (Joe, Ep10 series, July 2026).** In a multi-part series or a post with sequential beats, a hand-off like "Here is the second one" / "the next thing" / "and there's more" forces the reader to hold context and guess what's coming. Name it plainly instead. Joe's own fix: "Yesterday we cleared the money fear. Here is the second one." → "Yesterday we cleared the money fear. **Today we talk about the seller.**" He said directly: "See how much easier it is to understand?" Always state WHAT the next part is about, by name, in the transition line — never a bare positional pointer.
- **NAME THE SUBJECT — never leave it to guesswork (Joe, SM educational, July 2026; RE-FLAGGED Ep10 4-day series, July 2026).** If a word could be read two ways by a stranger, disambiguate it in the words themselves. The canonical catch: bare "storage" / "self-storage" reads to most people as *renting or using a unit*, NOT investing. When the piece is about INVESTING, always say "self-storage investing" / "self-storage investment" / "buy a self-storage facility" — on the poster art AND the caption, in the opening line AND the CTA. Joe: *"never leave anything to guesswork."* Apply the same test to any term whose plain reading differs from your intended meaning: if a stranger could guess wrong, spell it out. (Sibling to the SI "infer the reference, don't lift it raw" rule — both ensure the reader never has to decode or assume.)
  - **⛔ MANDATORY BARE-NOUN SWEEP (Joe re-corrected on the Ep10 series after I'd already been told once — do this EVERY time, do not wait to be asked).** Before any caption/poster ships, scan for EVERY bare noun a cold stranger could read wrong and pin it to self-storage explicitly. The repeat offenders: "first-timer" / "first-time buyer" → "first-time self-storage buyer"; "the deal" → "the storage deal" / "your first self-storage deal"; "the seller" → "the storage owner" / "the facility owner" (on first use); "the facility" is fine once storage is established; "buyers" → "storage buyers"; "the market" → "the self-storage market"; "brokers" → "storage brokers" (on first use). Establish "self-storage" in the FIRST line, then it's fine to say "the facility"/"the deal" once context is locked — but the opener and the CTA must never be bare. This is not optional polish; it is a standing gate Joe should never have to raise again.

### 9. NOT every post is promotional — vary the JOB of the piece (Joe corrected, SI Ep16 July 2026, ALL brands)

Joe's directive: "not every post is promotional, some are educative and some might be entertaining, so don't let all the posts read as promotional." A batch where every piece pushes the show/product/community reads like an ad wall and gets tuned out.

- **Decide the JOB of each piece before writing it.** Three modes:
  - **Educative** — teach one useful thing the reader keeps even if they never click. Give real value, no ask hiding in it.
  - **Entertaining** — a story, a sharp observation, a bit of dry humor that earns attention on its own.
  - **Promotional** — the one that actually drives to the episode / community / funnel.
- **Mix the modes across a batch.** Most pieces should educate or entertain and EARN the audience; only some carry the promotional ask. If every caption ends by selling the episode, the batch is miscalibrated — rebalance it.
- **Match the CTA to the job.** An educative or entertaining post can close soft or not push at all (the value IS the point). Save the full funnel road-map close for the promotional pieces. (Per-brand CTA rules still govern HOW the promotional close is worded.)
- This does not weaken the funnel — it strengthens it. A feed that gives before it asks earns the right to ask.

### 10. Problem → clear SOLUTION, and it must FLOW (Joe's four-question test, SI Ep16 July 2026, ALL brands)

Joe's own gate for every piece — run these four questions on each one, honestly, before it ships:

1. **What is the message this is passing? Is it clear?** (the stranger test, Rule 8)
2. **If it shows a PROBLEM — does it make me want the solution? If it gives a SOLUTION — does it make me want to learn more?** A piece that states a problem boldly and then ends on a vague reassurance ("it's fixable," "the problem disappears") has failed: it never showed the solution, so there's no pull. Show the actual solution or the vivid, specific change the reader gets — never a hollow "there's an answer" placeholder.
3. **Is it properly worded, good vocabulary — or is it just stitching things together?** (Rules 2, 3, 6)
4. **Does it flow?** Read it top to bottom — does each beat lead into the next, or does it dead-end / jump?

If a piece can't answer all four cleanly, it isn't done. This is the honest self-critique Joe expects instead of defending a draft.

### 11. ATTRIBUTE a personal story, result, or specific teaching to the person who said it (Joe, SM Ep04, July 2026, ALL brands)

If a post states a personal story, a result, a number, or a specific teaching that came from a particular person (a guest OR a host), name that person. Never put one person's story or claim in another person's mouth, and never present a guest's teaching as the narrator's own knowledge with no credit.

- **A host's own lived belief/story stays the host's** — e.g. Joe's verified "eight years ago I sat in a room certain a lender would never hand me a million dollars" is genuinely Joe's, so first person is correct. Verify against the Understanding/transcript before deciding whose it is — don't guess, and don't flip a correctly-attributed line just to "fix" it.
- **PREFERRED FORMAT = a direct QUOTE + dash attribution, not woven-in phrasing (Joe, Ep04).** When a personal belief/line belongs to someone, present it as a quote with the name after a dash: `"...the exact words..." - Joe Downs`. Joe explicitly rejected the woven-in style ("I had it backwards, and Jack Pezzino spelled out why on the episode") in favor of the clean quote-attribution. Put the quoted line first, credit the speaker, then let the teaching stand as plain copy.
- **A guest's teaching:** state the teaching plainly (it doesn't have to be a quote if it's general fact), but if you put a specific line in someone's mouth, quote-and-attribute it to them. Don't present one person's lived belief as another's, and don't drop attribution to tighten a caption.
- **Mixed post:** keep each beat with its true owner — the host's belief in first person, the guest's teaching credited to the guest, in the same post.
- **⛔ SCOPED EDIT: when the ask is "just credit the person," change ONLY the attribution — keep every other word verbatim (Joe corrected HARD, twice in one session, Ep04).** Joe gave approved SBA copy and asked only to attribute one line. I rewrote the entire caption ("Jack spelled out why…"), he rejected it, I rewrote it AGAIN a different way, he rejected that too: *"this was the copy why are you changing it this was better just credit the person who said it."* The correct move: take the approved text AS-IS, wrap the one line that belongs to someone in quotes + `- Name`, and touch NOTHING else. This is the attribution-edit case of the standing APPROVED-COPY-IS-LOCKED rule: an edit request scoped to X (attribution, a typo, one word, a CTA tail) means change X and leave the rest byte-for-byte. Do not "improve" surrounding copy you weren't asked to touch. If you think other lines also need work, ASK — don't unilaterally rewrite.
- **Verbatim quotes come from the SOURCE, not memory.** When you put a line in quotation marks and attribute it, the words inside the quotes must be what the person actually said — pull the transcript and use the exact words (Joe: "pull directly from the transcripts"). A paraphrase inside quote marks is a fabricated quote. If you only have a paraphrase, either fetch the real line or drop the quote marks and credit plainly.
- This is the brand-agnostic version of the per-episode "attribute correctly, never fabricate" rules in storagemoguls-podcast / successfulidiots-podcast. Same root as Rule 4 (faithful paraphrase, no invention): don't reassign who said/did a thing.

### 12. COMPLETENESS — every sentence must be whole, leaving nothing to questions (Joe corrected 3x in one session, SM Ep11 July 2026, ALL brands)

Joe's standing standard, stated by name: *"we agreed on completeness of sentences"* and *"leaving nothing to questions."* A sentence that raises an obvious "where? on what? by whom?" and doesn't answer it inside itself has failed — even if it parses and reads clean. This is stricter than Rule 5 (parses cold): a line can parse and still be *incomplete*.

Three failure sub-types, all caught in one Ep11 session:

- **DANGLING object — the claim names no target.** ❌ "Doing a survey is the reason I didn't lose $900,000." → lose it *where, on what?* ✅ "...the reason I didn't lose $900,000 **on a self-storage deal**." Name the thing the claim happened to, inside the sentence. Move the specific detail (which deal, which place) up so the opener is whole on its own.
- **VAGUE noun — a category where a stranger needs the specific.** ❌ "the mistake that costs **first-time buyers** their leverage" (first-time buyers of *what?* reads like first-time home buyers) → ✅ "**people buying their first self-storage facility**." Never bare "buyer" / "first-timer" / "deal" when the specific subject is the whole point. (This is the completeness face of Rule 8's "name the subject" — Rule 8 says disambiguate a word that reads two ways; Rule 12 says a sentence missing its specific object is incomplete, full stop.)
- **LOGIC INVERSION — the sentence states the opposite of what's meant.** ❌ "the report you **skip** is the one that saves you" (if you skipped it, it did NOT save you — logically broken) → ✅ "the report you **think you should skip** is the one that could actually save you." It's the *temptation* to skip, not the act. Also fix the verb that carries the causation: ❌ "**A survey** is the reason I didn't lose the money" → ✅ "**Doing a survey** is the reason..." (the ACTION saved the money, not the object). Read the literal cause-and-effect of every claim sentence — if taken literally it says the opposite of the point, rewrite.

**Test:** for every sentence, ask the questions a reader would naturally ask of it — *where? on what? which one? who? does this literally say what I mean?* If the sentence doesn't answer its own obvious question, or would be literally false read cold, it is incomplete. Complete it inside the sentence; don't rely on the next line or the reader's assumption.

This bites HARD in short forms and openers (where a dangling clause has no later sentence to lean on) and on poster sublines. Apply it to poster art too — a poster line must be a complete thought, not a fragment that raises a question the caption answers.

### 13. ALWAYS RE-READ your content COLD before handing it to Joe — no exceptions (Joe directive, Ep10 4-day series, July 2026, ALL brands)

Joe's standing rule, stated directly after catching three separate grammar/vocabulary misses in one batch ("They think it like this", "Not one is about", "every one disappears"): **before ANY content reaches him for review, read every line of it back cold, out loud in your head, as if a stranger wrote it and you are proofing it.** The dash-scan and bare-noun sweep are NOT a substitute for this — those catch specific tells; this catches broken English, clumsy stitches, wrong words, and lines that simply don't sound right.

- **This is a distinct, final pass — run it AFTER writing-craft + humanizer, on the assembled final copy, every piece, every platform variant.** Not a spot-check. Read the WHOLE thing top to bottom.
- **The test for each sentence:** does this sound like something a careful writer would actually write? Read it aloud. If any part makes you pause, re-read, or think "that's a little off," it IS off — rewrite it before Joe ever sees it.
- **Why it exists:** the recurring failure is shipping copy that passed the mechanical gates but still contains a phrase that doesn't parse or sounds tacky ("They think it like this" has no grammar behind it). Joe should never be the one catching these. His words: the writer re-reads their own work before submitting it.
- **Honesty clause:** when you tell Joe the content is ready, you are asserting you actually did this read. Never claim it if you didn't. If you re-read and found nothing, say what you scanned for; if you found and fixed something, that's the pass working.

The pipeline order is now: `source → understanding → draft (voice) → [WRITING-CRAFT] → [HUMANIZER] → CTA → [FINAL COLD RE-READ] → output`. The cold re-read is the last thing before it leaves your hands.

### 14. ACCURACY vs the SOURCE — never state a claim backwards or against the thesis (Joe corrected HARD, Ep10 4-day series, July 2026, ALL brands)

The most damaging miss this session was not grammar — it was **content accuracy**. I wrote copy that was clean, humanized, and on-brand but said the OPPOSITE of what the episode taught. Joe caught it directly: *"is it really money according to the understanding... some of the statements feel made up."* Grammar gates do NOT catch this. This rule does.

Three accuracy failures from one batch, all avoidable by checking against the Understanding/transcript:

- **THESIS INVERSION (the big one).** The Ep10 episode's whole point is that money is NOT the real barrier — it only *feels* that way; the barrier is three fears. I opened Day 1 with *"The number one reason people never buy their first facility is money."* That states the exact opposite of the source thesis. FIX: *"Most people think money is what stops them. It almost never is."* **Before writing, name the piece's core claim in one sentence and confirm it MATCHES the source's thesis, not contradicts it.** An opener that argues against the episode is a fabrication even if every word is grammatical.
- **DIRECTION REVERSAL of a real line.** Joe's actual line: *"the financing fear is a math fear dressed up as a credibility fear."* I wrote *"a math fear wearing a credibility costume"* — which reverses which thing is the disguise. When you paraphrase a specific line, check the DIRECTION of the relationship (X dressed as Y, not Y dressed as X), not just the words.
- **DROPPED NUANCE that changes the fact.** Source: a retrade for a *genuine material* due-diligence finding is fine; it's the *bait-and-switch* squeeze that burns you. I wrote a flat *"you don't retrade"* — which is inaccurate and would misteach the reader. When the source qualifies a claim, carry the qualifier; a stripped claim can become false.

**The check (run BEFORE the cold re-read, on every piece):** for every factual claim, number, and framing in the copy, point to WHERE in the Understanding/transcript it comes from and confirm the copy says the SAME thing the source says — same direction, same nuance, same thesis. If a line "feels made up," it usually is: either it drifted from the source or it was invented for punch (see Rule 4 and joe-cta's Overclaim Trap). Fold this into Rule 13's final pass: the cold re-read now checks FOUR things Joe named — **flow, accuracy of content, grammar, and ease of reading** — not grammar alone.

### 15. STRUCTURE a teaching/educational piece — set the stage before the point, close firm (Joe, Ep10 series, July 2026, ALL brands)

Joe rejected teaching drafts that "just dived in without anything" (stated the lesson before framing what the piece is about) and that "left people hanging" (ended without a solution or firm statement). The fix is a STRUCTURE layer that sits on top of these rules. Full spine + template + the two tests are in `references/teaching-caption-structure.md`. The non-negotiables:
- **The POINT never appears before the HAND-OFF line.** Open by setting the stage (what is this, who is it for, why care), THEN a flat hand-off line that points forward, THEN the lesson. If the lesson is in the first line, it dived in cold.
- **Myth-busting order:** topic → belief (stated in the reader's words AND validated, never mocked) → turn → truth + the mechanism/why → concrete proof → firm close.
- **The last non-CTA line is always a firm CLOSE** (a solution or a screenshot-able summary that resolves the opener's tension), never the problem restated or a hollow "it's fixable." The CTA is separate and comes after; the lesson must feel finished before the invite.
- **Two tests:** (a) does the reader know what this is and why it matters BEFORE the point lands? (b) read the last non-CTA line cold, does the lesson feel landed or did you just stop talking?

## Where this runs in the pipeline
```
source → understanding → draft (voice) → [WRITING-CRAFT pass] → [humanizer pass] → CTA → [FINAL COLD RE-READ] → output
```

Writing-craft comes AFTER voice (so you're fixing real Joe/brand-voiced copy) and BEFORE humanizer (so the de-AI pass polishes already-correct writing, not broken writing). The FINAL COLD RE-READ (Rule 13) is the last step before output — read the whole assembled thing back cold, every time. All gates are mandatory; none replaces the other.

## Self-check before handing off (every piece, every platform variant)

- [ ] Every unclear-standing-alone span was inferred/paraphrased to its real meaning (not lifted verbatim).
- [ ] No stitched phrases — every clause parses cold.
- [ ] Every word is the correct word for the meaning; nothing invented or mis-sensed.
- [ ] Paraphrase is faithful (clarifies meaning, adds no facts).
- [ ] Every line answers/advances the one before it (Rule 6) — no sentence merely sitting beside the prior one, answering an adjacent question, or negating its own beat.
- [ ] Read each line with zero context — it makes clean, correct sense.
- [ ] MESSAGE CLARITY (Rule 8): a stranger with zero context gets the point in about a second — no clever labels, no decoding.
- [ ] POST JOB (Rule 9): this piece's job (educate / entertain / promote) is decided, and the batch isn't all-promotional.
- [ ] FOUR-QUESTION TEST (Rule 10): message clear? problem makes me want the solution / solution makes me want more (no hollow "it's fixable")? properly worded, not stitched? does it flow?
- [ ] ATTRIBUTION (Rule 11): any personal story/result/teaching is credited to who actually said it (quote + "- Name" for a personal line, verbatim from source); and if the ask was scoped ("just credit the person" / fix one thing), ONLY that changed, rest is verbatim.
- [ ] COMPLETENESS (Rule 12): every sentence answers its own obvious question (where? on what? which one?), names the specific subject (never bare "buyer"/"deal"), and is literally TRUE read cold (no "the report you skip saves you" inversions).
- [ ] ACCURACY vs SOURCE (Rule 14): every claim/number/framing traces to the Understanding/transcript and says the SAME thing (same direction, same nuance, same thesis) — no line argues against the episode, reverses a real line, or drops a qualifier that makes it false.
- [ ] FINAL COLD RE-READ (Rule 13): the WHOLE assembled piece was read back top to bottom, out loud in your head, as if proofing a stranger's work, checking all four things Joe named — flow, accuracy of content, grammar, ease of reading. This is the last thing done before handing to Joe, every time.

State briefly what you checked. If a line fails, rewrite it; don't ship it and hope.

## Maintenance
When a new writing-quality failure shows up in ANY brand's content (wrong word, mis-paraphrase, nonsense stitch), log the example and the fix HERE — not in the individual brand skill — so the correction propagates to every brand at once. This is the whole point of the skill being shared.
