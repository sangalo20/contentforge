## 🔒 HARD RULE — TRUTH + STORAGE-DOMAIN AUDIT GATE (run BEFORE building any doc)

Joe set this directly (June 2026) after two fabricated/wrong posts — an invented "first deal" origin story and a "month-to-month tenants who could leave next week" risk line — passed through this review step into his doc and nearly published. The earlier failure was that this skill copied source copy through FAITHFULLY without auditing it: it assumed whatever was in the generator output / Metricool / Notion was already clean. It wasn't. The no-fabrication rule lived only in the generator skills, so content that traveled any other path was never checked.

**This gate closes that gap: EVERY piece that lands in a review doc gets audited HERE, at build time, no matter where it came from** (linkedin-posts, joe-from-podcasts, the brand podcast skills, Metricool drafts, Notion, or copy handed over by the agency). The review doc is the single funnel everything passes through before it reaches Joe — so the audit must live here too, not only upstream.

For each post (long form AND short form) before it goes in the doc:
1. **Fabrication check** — every first-person sentence (a story, deal, memory, number, client, result, origin) must trace to a real source (soul.md, Notion, a transcript, a verified Joe fact). Plausible ≠ true. If it can't be traced, it is fabricated — cut it, reframe it as idea-only/opinion, or mark it inline `⚠ VERIFY: <what to confirm>` so Joe sees the flag in his own doc. NEVER pass an unverifiable first-person claim through clean.
2. **Storage-domain check** — the claim must be true *for self-storage*, not just true-sounding for real estate. Known trap: **month-to-month is universal in storage = normal, never a risk/instability flag.** Catch domain errors even when they aren't fabrications (a true-sounding but storage-wrong line).
3. **Provenance** — a guest's story/result belongs to the guest, attributed, never transplanted into Joe's mouth as lived experience.

If a piece fails the gate, do NOT silently fix it AND do NOT silently pass it — either correct it to a sourced/true version, or surface it to Joe with the `⚠ VERIFY` flag in the doc and a note in the run summary. The default end state of a run includes telling Joe which pieces were flagged or changed and why.

## The Pipeline (locked rules)

1. Generate the week's batch with `linkedin-posts`.
2. Place the batch into a **Google Doc** named: `Week {N}: {Mon}–{Sun, Year} — LinkedIn Articles` (ISO week number). **If the batch is sourced from another brand/podcast rather than Joe's own native content, append the source brand in parentheses to the title** — e.g. `… — LinkedIn Articles (Successful Idiots)` or `(Storage Moguls)`. Joe's own native content (straight from `linkedin-posts`) gets NO suffix. This is a filename-level provenance tag so anyone scanning the Drive folder sees at a glance which docs are borrowed-source vs. Joe-native.

> ⚠️ **The suffix is PROVENANCE, never a posting destination.** `(Storage Moguls)` / `(Successful Idiots)` means *the insight came from that podcast* — it does NOT mean the post goes to that brand's account. Every post in these review docs is first-person Joe voice = **Joe Downs PERSONAL account**. A downstream session (June 2026) mis-routed these to the SM/SI brand planners by reading the suffix as a destination — Joe corrected it. Brand-account posts are a separate set (3rd-person brand voice from the podcast skills) and live in Notion. When scheduling, confirm `Account` from Notion's Content Pieces DB, not from the doc title.
3. Each post becomes its **own section** in the doc: Heading-1 headline + italic theme label + full post text, with a **page break** between sections and a clickable **Contents** (TOC) at the top.

### HARD RULE — Tag the source of borrowed content
When a post's insight is NOT Joe's direct/first-hand experience — i.e. it came from another brand, podcast, or person (e.g. a `joe-from-podcasts` post drawn from Peter Swain / Successful Idiots) — the source brand MUST be named **both** in the doc title (parenthetical suffix, see rule 2) **and** inline per article (fold a `Source: <Brand> <Ep#> (…)` clause into each article's theme label so it rides through the TOC and the section header). This protects Joe: the reviewer can instantly see provenance and that a claim belongs to a guest, not to Joe. Joe-native posts need no source clause.
4. **Joe** reviews and emails the external partner himself (unless he explicitly asks this skill to share/send).
5. The external partner approves.
6. **Only after Joe gives explicit verbal approval** does the agent mark the topic in Notion (SM Content Tracker).

### HARD RULE — Do NOT touch Notion
Never update, check boxes on, or otherwise modify the Notion SM Content Tracker as part of this skill. Notion is marked **only** when Joe personally and explicitly says the content is approved. Until then, Notion is read-only.

### HARD RULE — Joe reviews before anything is sent
Joe ALWAYS reviews the doc himself before it goes to the external partner. The agent never emails, shares, or sends the doc to the partner on its own initiative. Default end state of a run = doc built + link reported to Joe, full stop. Only share/send if Joe explicitly says "share it" / "email it" in that moment — and even then, confirm recipient + role/subject first.

## WRITING PRINCIPLES (Joe taught these — apply from the START, not as after-the-fact fixes)

These are durable craft rules Joe corrected me into over a long iterative session. The meta-lesson: **treat every correction as a principle to carry forward**, not a one-off patch on this doc. Write from UNDERSTANDING the idea, then say it well — don't assemble words and hope.

1. **Write from understanding — never compress.** The X short form is NOT the long-form with words cut. Understand the ONE core insight, then rebuild it native to X: a hook first line that stops the scroll, line breaks for air, a single punch close, and it must stand alone (a reader who never sees the long post gets a complete thought). No "follow along," no "Joe Downs, CEO of…" sign-off — that's LinkedIn furniture. A patched/compressed tweet reads like a summary; a rebuilt one reads like a person thinking.

2. **An image only lands when paired with its payoff in the SAME beat.** A vivid detail ("no story to tell at dinner," "a row of metal units off a county road") does nothing as a stranded fragment in a list of costs. Set the cost directly against the reward so the reader FEELS the trade: *"Boring at the party. Rich in the bank. I'll take that trade every time."* Cost → payoff, back to back. That's what makes "unglamorous but smart" actually feel smart.

3. **Attribution must flow — never bolt it on.** Do NOT credit someone in a trailing sentence or parenthetical ("(from Peter Swain)") or a footnote ("The Italian example is Peter's, not mine") — it stops the post dead to do bookkeeping. Instead weave WHO the person is into the thought at FIRST mention, as an appositive in Joe's own voice: *"Peter Swain, my Successful Idiots co-host, put it to me like this:"* / *"My Successful Idiots co-host Peter Swain nailed the fix:"*. Then let the post END on its real point. You still must identify anyone named (the reader doesn't know them) — but as Joe would say it, never a formal bio title.

4. **Stay true to the knowledge — invent nothing.** Every stat, number, and claim must exist in the source (the real scheduled post / transcript / Notion record). No fabricated figures to make a line land. (Verified facts available: $800K→$1.4M operations gap, 60% of searches end with no click, 100 units/+$10/~3% churn, the Italian-proposal example.)

5. **Maintain Joe's voice always.** Plain, declarative, lived-in, a little contrarian. Names the unglamorous truth flat, then flips it into the edge. Short sentences. No hype adjectives. He earns the point; he doesn't announce it.

6. **Don't out-clever the source.** The biggest voice failure mode: writing a PUNCHIER, more clever version of Joe's point than the long form actually makes (e.g. a rhyming payoff like "Boring at the party. Rich in the bank." when the long form just says "I'll take the unglamorous facility that prints cash"). That cleverness is the agent's voice, not Joe's — he's plainer. The X post must stay WITHIN what the long form claims and in his register: reuse HIS actual lines/phrases ("That's the whole point," "prints cash for twenty years," "the right people in the room"), don't invent a zinger. Also don't drop the idea's real heart to fit a tighter close — e.g. "First deal" is ABOUT having the right people in the room, so that beat stays even under the 280 budget. When trimming for length, cut words, not the point.

**Person identifiers (verified):** Peter Swain = Joe's co-host on the Successful Idiots podcast. Use that as his in-post identifier.

12. **NEVER fabricate Joe's personal history, origin, or first-deal story — and NEVER let generic real-estate language create a storage domain error (Joe corrected BOTH, June 2026, on already-SCHEDULED posts).** This is the sharpest edge of #4 ("invent nothing"). #4 is usually read as "don't invent stats/numbers" — but the two worst failures this session were NOT numbers:
> - **Invented origin.** A post asserted *"I got in because I watched people chase exciting deals and lose money… my first deal was a small facility in a market most investors would've skipped."* Joe: *"that's not how my first deal went or why I got into storage."* A constructed-but-plausible biography is STILL a fabrication, and it's worse than a wrong stat because it's in first person and reads as testimony. RULE: never assert a specific origin story, first-deal detail, motivation, or personal-history fact unless it traces to something Joe actually said/wrote (transcript, prior approved post, Notion). If the angle needs an origin and you don't have the real one, either (a) write it WITHOUT inventing specifics (make the point without claiming a particular deal), or (b) flag to Joe: *"I don't have your real first-deal story — give me one line and I'll build it true,"* and hold. Do NOT fill the gap with invention.
> - **Domain error from leaked generic-RE language.** A rent-roll post listed *"month-to-month tenants who could leave next week"* as a hidden risk. Joe: *"month-to-month as an issue doesn't make sense. All storage tenants are month-to-month."* He's right — in **self-storage, month-to-month is UNIVERSAL and normal**, never a red flag. That framing is correct for apartments/commercial (where leases are the standard and a M2M tenant signals instability), and it leaked in from generic real-estate diligence templates. RULE: storage copy must be written from STORAGE reality, not generic-RE diligence boilerplate. Before shipping any "diligence / what to watch for" storage post, sanity-check each item against how storage actually works — M2M leases, tenant insurance, delinquency/auction process, unit mix, occupancy economics. If a "risk" would only be a risk in a leased asset class, cut it.
>
> Meta-rule: these errors rode all the way into SCHEDULED Metricool posts undetected. Domain-correctness and personal-truth are not polish — they're a hard gate that runs on EVERY storage/Joe-history post before it leaves drafting, the same as the length and humanizer gates.

### Diagnostic — when Joe flags a published/scheduled post as wrong, TRACE the source before answering (Joe, June 2026)
Joe's real question when he flags bad copy is usually *"I thought you pulled from our data — how is it wrong?"* Do NOT guess or apologize generically. Trace the exact post to its true origin first:
1. Pull the live scheduled posts from **Metricool** (`getScheduledPosts`, Joe Downs blogId **6425321**) and find the matching `text` + `id` + `creatorUserMail` + `creationDate`.
2. Compare against the review Doc (read it back via `google_api.py docs get <id>`).
3. Determine WHERE the error entered: was the review-doc step faithful (it copied what was already scheduled), or did the doc/agent introduce it? Most often the doc faithfully mirrors copy that was **already written and scheduled** by another account (e.g. `alvin@belroseam.com`), meaning the error lives in the SOURCE drafting, not the pipeline step.
4. Answer Joe with the honest finding: name which posts, what's wrong, exactly where it entered, and own the sourcing-discipline failure plainly (don't deflect to "the data"). Then offer the concrete fix + a pause/pull if the posts are time-sensitive (publishing today). Joe values owning errors plainly far more than a clean-sounding excuse.

7. **One coherent theme beats two half-landed ones — and that OVERRIDES \"keep every beat\" when they collide (Joe's FINAL call this session).** Principle #6 says don't drop the idea's real heart to fit a tighter close. That holds WHEN the whole idea can be expressed coherently. But when a post genuinely carries two ideas and 280 chars can't set both up properly, do NOT gesture at both and land neither — **pick the ONE major theme and let it land cleanly.** The test Joe applied (read it COLD, as someone who never saw the long form): if a beat appears with no setup it reads as a non-sequitur and breaks the post. Worked example: \"First deal / the right people in the room\" tries to carry (a) boring deals quietly pay and (b) you need the right people. In 280 chars, tacking \"It was the right people in the room\" onto an all-about-the-deal post made the people line land from nowhere — Joe flagged it as making no sense. The fix was to commit the X version to ONE theme (boring deals pay) and cut the people angle entirely, rather than keep a stranded fragment of it. So: prefer keeping all beats (#6); but if they won't cohere in the space, choosing one theme that fully lands > preserving a beat that arrives unsupported. Flag to Joe which theme you chose when you drop the other (especially if the dropped one is in the idea's name), in case he wants the post rebuilt around the other theme instead. Still NEVER invent or patch to force both in — understanding, not assembly.

8. **Length is a budget to write WITHIN — never hack a finished post down to fit.** The 280 cap is a constraint you write toward from the start, not a guillotine. The failure mode Joe caught: trimming words to hit 280 and cutting LOAD-BEARING clauses, so the post is the right length but the logic breaks (e.g. "David didn't beat Goliath with size. He had something Goliath never saw coming." — but the post never said WHAT that something was, so it argues nothing; or a metaphor opener whose setup got cut so it reads as a riddle). Before trimming, identify which clauses carry the logic vs. which are redundant words — cut ONLY redundant words. If all beats won't fit, COMBINE beats into tighter sentences or rephrase, but never delete a step the conclusion depends on. Final gate: read each X post cold, as someone who never saw the long form — does it make complete sense and land its own point? If not, it's not done, regardless of length.

9. **DERIVE the X post from the long form by LIFTING Joe's actual sentences — do NOT re-author (Joe corrected this hardest).** The long-form copy is the source of truth: already his voice, already approved-quality. The worst failure mode is re-writing it into "cleaner" or "punchier" phrasing — that introduces meaning drift and invented lines. Real examples of drift Joe caught: changed "because it was exciting" → "for the excitement" (flips the meaning); added "the rent showed up every month ANYWAY" (defensive tone not in original); INVENTED "David finally has the slingshot" (nowhere in source — the source says "he had access to something Goliath didn't see coming"); dropped "or something" from "while someone, or something, watches" (the "or something" = AI, central to Joe's theme). The method: SELECT which of Joe's real sentences to keep and in what order, paste them VERBATIM, and fit 280 by DROPPING whole beats (keep the logic-essential ones) — not by paraphrasing his sentences shorter. "Redundant" is a dangerous judgment call: some seemingly-redundant words carry context/tone — when unsure, keep them and drop a different beat instead. Trim decorative tails ("on every one," "too many") before touching meaning-bearing words. After trimming, re-read against the source to confirm zero drift.

10. **A curiosity QUESTION only works if its answer is something the reader WANTS — a trivia question about the topic fails (Joe corrected this, SM Ep09 June 2026).** When a hook or CTA opens with a question to pull the reader in, the question must point at the reader's real desire — *"can I do this? how does this actually work? is this within reach for someone like me?"* — NOT trivia about the subject. Joe rejected *"Ever wonder who actually owns the storage place down the road?"* with *"how does this help, who owns the storage is that really what we talk about"* — it's trivia, it promises nothing, so it doesn't earn the read. The STRUCTURE (question → reason-to-follow → where-it-gets-answered) was sound; only the question was empty. The test: would a curious stranger actually care about the answer, and does the post/community then DELIVER that answer? Good replacements paid off: *"Ever wonder how two people in their 20s actually pull this off?"*, *"Is owning storage actually within reach for someone like you?"*, *"Ever wonder how you'd spot a problem like this before you bought?"* Frame the destination as where that question gets answered ("where people go from wondering to owning").

11. **A "follow / subscribe" ask is a REASON, not an instruction — and the close must read conversational, not rushed (Joe, SM Ep09, three correction rounds).** Don't write "follow us so the next one finds you" (clunky instruction). State the RHYTHM and let the follow be self-motivated: "New episodes every Tuesday." / "We do this every Tuesday." Calibrate LENGTH to a conversational middle: Joe rejected the wordy version AND then the over-tightened one (*"Too tight now, sounds like we are in a rush"*). Two short sentences that breathe — if it reads like you're hurrying, it's wrong; if it takes three clauses to reach the point, also wrong. When alternating a batch, vary warm (soft curiosity question) ↔ normal (crisp framing line) per post so the feed never repeats. And when Joe flags only ONE section (e.g. "the follow + community area"), change ONLY that block — leave the rest of the copy verbatim; don't rewrite the whole thing.

> **RESEARCH RATIONALE (condensed, pulled June 2026 — durable):** Question hooks engage through self-reflection and suit educational / problem-solving content (the typical audience for Joe's brands); curiosity-GAP / mystery hooks are for viral reach and carry clickbait risk — use sparingly. CTAs convert on *action + a reason + clear value + low friction*; the lowest-friction micro-commitment (follow) goes FIRST, the bigger ask (visit the site / join) SECOND, each naming what the reader gets. A generic ordered command ("join now") underperforms a value-named, frictionless path. This is why #10/#11 work: small ask first, value named, and the question must promise something the reader actually wants.

### ⚠️ FINAL format Joe locked (June 2026) — LONG FORM + SHORT FORM, two variants per idea
The format went through three states this session; the LAST one is authoritative. Joe found reviewing four near-identical platform variants per idea too tiresome, so he collapsed them to TWO review variants per idea:
- **LONG FORM** — ONE version, used for **LinkedIn · Instagram · Facebook** (all three take the same long-form copy). This is the existing/"real" copy that's already written — **leave it as-is, don't rewrite it.**
- **SHORT FORM** — ONE version, created fresh for **X** (tight, punchy, ≤ 280 chars). This is the only new copy you write per idea.

So each idea block shows exactly **2 copies to review** (long + short), not four. The intermediate "write the caption ONCE, no per-platform variants" rule is RETIRED — and so is the four-way LinkedIn/IG-feed/IG-story/X breakout. Don't reintroduce either.

**Platform listing rule (Joe directive):** ALWAYS list all four — **LinkedIn · Instagram · Facebook · X** — even though Joe's accounts currently have ONLY LinkedIn + Instagram connected (no Facebook, no X wired up). List the intent, not just the connected set. (Where a true coverage gap exists — e.g. a Story with no matching feed idea — flag it with a ⚠ gap note, but still list all four platforms.)

**Per-idea block layout, top to bottom (this order):**
1. `IDEA {n} — <idea name>` header (amber bar)
2. ☐ `APPROVED` checkbox (Joe confirmed he wants an in-doc checkbox per block)
3. 📲 `GOES TO:` `LinkedIn · Instagram · Facebook · X`
4. `── LONG FORM (LinkedIn · Instagram · Facebook) · <date/time ET> ──` then the long-form copy
5. `── SHORT FORM (X) · <date/time ET> ──` then the short-form copy
6. optional ⚠ gap note (e.g. an IG Story still to be created)
7. `VISUAL` →
   - `TYPE:` one of **Poster / Photo / Carousel / Reel / Video**
   - Poster/Photo → image embedded inline. Carousel → ALL images embedded in order. Reel/Video → a clickable **video link** (Google Docs cannot embed playable video; only a link).

**Attribution (Joe directive — he corrected this HARD, multiple times, June 2026):** name the specific person (e.g. Peter Swain) inside the copy ONLY when the idea/story is genuinely theirs — their framing, their example. Joe's own ideas need no name. Don't strip a real attribution; don't manufacture one.

> ⚠️ **HOW you attribute matters as much as whether. WEAVE the identifier into the sentence; never bolt it on.** Joe rejected every "tacked-on" form:
> - ❌ A separate admin sentence after the point: *"…Peter said recently. Peter and I host the Successful Idiots podcast together."* (dead stop, reads like a disclaimer)
> - ❌ A trailing footnote: *"…that changes who gets found. The Italian example is Peter's, not mine."* (kills the closing punch to do bookkeeping)
> - ❌ A formal title bolted on: *"Peter Swain, founder of A3 Finance, …"* (corporate, not Joe's voice)
> - ❌ An X parenthetical credit tag: *"…very different results. (from Peter Swain, my SI co-host)"* (still a bolt-on)
>
> ✅ **The fix = the APPOSITIVE.** Introduce the person with a short descriptive phrase set off by commas, INSIDE the sentence already doing the work, at FIRST mention — so the reader learns who they are in passing and the line keeps moving:
> - *"A line from a recent conversation with my Successful Idiots co-host, Peter Swain, has been stuck in my head."* (woven into the hook)
